## 
This Mount Kenya North Face climb via Kamweti is a 10-day expedition for experienced rock climbers. You walk in on the quiet Kamweti trail through bamboo and moorland, summit Point Lenana (4,985 m) on day 5 to acclimatise, then spend two days on the classic North Face route to Batian (5,199 m), Kenya’s highest point. You walk out on the Chogoria side and return to Nairobi on day 10. 
It suits climbers who lead or second comfortably at around UIAA grade IV, can abseil on their own and want a full mountain journey rather than a quick summit. 

## Why choose the Kamweti route to the North Face? 
Kamweti is one of Mount Kenya’s least-used approaches, so you share the forest and the high valleys with very few other parties. The slow, three-day walk-in through forest, the Sagana and Thegu river valleys and past Lake Hohnel gives your body time to adjust before you reach the high huts. 
Climbing Point Lenana before the technical days is deliberate. A night at around 4,200 m and a summit near 5,000 m make the long day on the North Face safer and more enjoyable. 

## What to expect on the Mount Kenya North Face climb via Kamweti 
Days 1 to 4 are trekking days of about 5 to 7 hours, camping in the forest and moorland and reaching Mackinder’s Camp below the peaks. Day 5 starts before dawn for Point Lenana, then you settle at Shipton’s Camp. On day 6 a short walk takes you to Kami Hut at the foot of the North Face. 
Day 7 is the main climbing day: roughly ten rope lengths of rock up to a bivouac near Firmin Tower. On day 8 you finish to the summit of Batian around midday, abseil down and walk back to Shipton’s. Days 9 and 10 take you over Simba Col, down the Chogoria side and back to Nairobi. 

### When is the best time for the North Face? 
The North Face gets the sun and is usually in its best condition from late June to early October. January and February are dry too, but that season favours the south-east face routes on Nelion. Avoid the long rains from April to May.

Day 1 : 
Nairobi to Kamweti Forest Camp 

Leave Nairobi in the morning for the southern slopes of Mount Kenya. After lunch near the forest edge, walk about three hours through montane forest to your first camp near the old Kamweti forest station, at around 2,650 m. 

Day 2 : 
Kamweti to Sagana River Camp 

A long day of about 5 to 6 hours through bamboo and into the hagenia woodland, following the trail up to camp beside the Sagana River at roughly 3,150 m. Look out for signs of elephant and buffalo in the forest. 

Day 3 : 
Sagana River to Thegu River Camp 

Climb out of the forest onto open moorland with giant heather and the first views of the peaks. After about 6 hours you camp by the Thegu River at about 3,750 m. 

Day 4 : 
Thegu River to Mackinder's Camp via Lake Hohnel 

Walk past Lake Hohnel, a quiet tarn below the peaks, and continue up the Teleki Valley to Mackinder's Camp at about 4,200 m. Rest and an early dinner prepare you for the summit start. 

Day 5 : 
Point Lenana summit, then Shipton's Camp 

Start around 3 a.m. to reach Point Lenana (4,985 m) for sunrise. After photos, descend to Shipton's Camp at about 4,200 m for a well-earned rest. 

Day 6 : 
Shipton's Camp to Kami Hut 

A short walk of about two hours brings you to Kami Hut at the foot of the North Face. Your climbing guide checks gear, briefs the route and you rest for the climb. 

Day 7 : 
North Face climb to the bivouac 

Leave early for the base of the face and start climbing at first light. You climb roughly ten rope lengths of rock and spend the night at a bivouac near Firmin Tower. 

Day 8 : 
Batian summit and descent to Shipton's Camp 

Finish the climb to Batian (5,199 m), usually around midday. After the summit you abseil down the route and walk back to Shipton's Camp for dinner and a long sleep. 

Day 9 : 
Shipton's Camp over Simba Col to Chogoria 

Cross Simba Col and descend past the Gorges Valley to the Chogoria side, about 7 hours of walking. Overnight in bandas near the Chogoria park gate. 

Day 10 : 
Chogoria to Nairobi 

After breakfast, a 4x4 takes you down to Chogoria town and your transfer continues to Nairobi, arriving in the afternoon.

How hard is the Mount Kenya North Face climb via Kamweti? 

The Mount Kenya North Face climb via Kamweti is a serious technical route. Expect rock pitches up to about UIAA grade IV and IV+, long days above 4,500 m and abseiling on the descent. It is only suitable for experienced rock climbers who are fit and used to multi-pitch routes. 

Do I need my own climbing gear? 

Yes. Bring your own harness, helmet, rock shoes or stiff boots, belay and abseil device and personal slings. Group ropes and protection are provided by your climbing guide. Trekking gear and sleeping bags can be hired in Nanyuki. 

When is the best time to climb Mount Kenya's North Face? 

The North Face is usually best from late June to early October, when it is in the sun and the rock is drier. January and February are also dry, but the face can hold more ice. We avoid April, May and November because of the rains. 

Why does the climb include Point Lenana first? 

Summiting Point Lenana (4,985 m) on day 5 helps you acclimatise before the technical climb. Arriving at Kami Hut after a night at altitude and a near-5,000 m summit makes the long North Face day safer. 

Can this climb be arranged for a group or club? 

Yes. We regularly plan climbs for clubs and groups of friends. Rope teams are kept to two climbers per guide, so larger groups get extra climbing guides. Contact us with your numbers and dates.
