## 
This calving season photo safari runs from January to March in northern Tanzania. During these months the migration herds gather on the Ndutu plains in the southern Serengeti ecosystem, and huge numbers of wildebeest calves are born within a few weeks. 
For photographers, this is one of the most exciting times of year. Newborn calves, curious young zebras and very active lions, cheetahs and hyenas give you action and emotion in the same frame. 

## Why choose this calving season photo safari? 
The short green grass at Ndutu makes for clean backgrounds and clear views. Three nights in the area give you time to find a birth, follow a hunt or wait for cheetahs to climb a termite mound. 
- Ndutu plains: newborn calves, herds and dramatic summer storms. 
- Predators: lions, cheetahs, hyenas and jackals following the herds. 
- Ngorongoro Crater: a full day on the crater floor with lions, rhinos and flamingos. 
- Central Serengeti: rocky kopjes, leopards in sausage trees and big skies. 

## The route 
You start in Arusha and visit Tarangire for elephants and baobabs. Then you descend into Ngorongoro Crater before heading to Ndutu for three nights. The last two nights are in the central Serengeti, before a short flight back to Arusha. 

### Shooting in the green season 
Storm clouds build in the afternoon, which gives dramatic light. Bring a rain cover for your camera and a lens cloth. Mornings are usually clear and calm, perfect for backlit calves in the grass. 

## Best time for a calving season photo safari 
Calving usually peaks from late January to February, but the herds are on the southern plains from about December to March. Rain decides where the herds graze, so your guide will follow the latest reports. Our Serengeti migration guide explains the yearly cycle.

Day 1 : 
Arrival in Arusha 

Meet and greet at Kilimanjaro International Airport and transfer to your lodge in Arusha. Rest and prepare your gear for the safari ahead. 

Day 2 : 
Arusha to Tarangire National Park 

Drive to Tarangire for a full day of game viewing among baobab trees and large elephant herds. Late afternoon, continue to Karatu in the highlands for the night. 

Day 3 : 
Ngorongoro Crater to Ndutu 

Descend into Ngorongoro Crater at sunrise for lions, black rhinos, buffaloes and flamingos on the soda lake. After a picnic lunch on the crater floor, drive west to the Ndutu area. 

Day 4 : 
Calving season photography at Ndutu 

Leave at first light to find the herds. Spend the day photographing newborn calves, mothers and the predators that follow them, with a packed lunch in the field. 

Day 5 : 
Full day on the Ndutu plains 

Another full day with the migration herds. Your guide follows the latest sightings of cheetahs and lions, and waits with you for the action to begin. 

Day 6 : 
Ndutu to central Serengeti 

A final morning on the calving plains, then drive north into the Serengeti. Afternoon game drive around the Seronera valley, a good area for leopards. 

Day 7 : 
Central Serengeti kopjes and rivers 

A full day exploring the kopjes, rivers and open plains of the central Serengeti, with time to wait for big cats in the best light. 

Day 8 : 
Fly back to Arusha 

Early morning game drive on the way to the airstrip, then a short scheduled flight back to Arusha in time for your onward journey.

When is the calving season in Tanzania? 

Calving usually peaks from late January to February on the Ndutu plains. The herds stay in the southern Serengeti from around December to March, depending on the rains. 

Where is Ndutu? 

Ndutu is on the border of the Serengeti National Park and the Ngorongoro Conservation Area in northern Tanzania. Its short-grass plains are where the herds give birth. 

Will I see predators hunting on the calving season photo safari? 

Very likely. Lions, cheetahs and hyenas follow the herds closely during calving, so hunts are common. Sightings can never be guaranteed. 

Does it rain during the calving season? 

Short showers and afternoon storms are common. Mornings are usually clear, and the storms make dramatic skies for photography. 

What camera gear do I need? 

Bring a long zoom such as 150-600 mm, a mid-range zoom for herds, a wide lens for skies, rain covers and plenty of batteries and memory cards. 

How do I get back to Arusha at the end? 

On day 8 you take a scheduled light aircraft flight from the central Serengeti back to Arusha, which saves a full day of driving. 

Can I combine this safari with Zanzibar? 

Yes. Many travellers fly to Zanzibar from the Serengeti or Arusha for a few days on the beach. We can arrange the extension.
