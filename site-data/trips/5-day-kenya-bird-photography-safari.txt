## 
This Kenya bird photography safari is made for photographers who love birds. It visits four Rift Valley lakes in five days, each with different habitats and species, and the drives between them are short, so you spend more time shooting. 
Kenya has well over 1,000 recorded bird species. The Rift Valley lakes hold a big share of them, from water birds and raptors to the bright little bush birds that make perfect close-ups. 

## Why choose this Kenya bird photography safari? 
Bird photography needs patience and the right position. Your guide knows where the birds feed and roost, and the vehicle is private, so you can wait as long as you need for a bird to land or take off. 
- Lake Naivasha: boat rides close to African fish eagles as they swoop for fish. 
- Lake Nakuru: flamingos, pelicans and rhinos on the same shoreline. 
- Lake Bogoria: hot springs, geysers and, in good years, huge flocks of lesser flamingos. 
- Lake Baringo: a freshwater lake with herons, kingfishers and cliff-dwelling owls. 

## Birds you can photograph 
Expect lilac-breasted rollers, superb starlings, kingfishers, hornbills, weavers and many raptors. Water birds include goliath herons, pelicans, cormorants and jacanas. At Baringo, local bird guides know the roosts of owls and nightjars. 
Flamingo numbers move between lakes depending on water levels, so your guide will check the latest reports. Read our Lake Bogoria guide and Lake Nakuru safari guide for more on each lake. 

## Best time for a Kenya bird photography safari 
Birding is good all year. From October to April, migrant birds from Europe and Asia join the resident species, and many birds wear breeding colours during the rains. The dry months from June to September give easier roads and clear light. 

### Gear for bird photography 
- A 150-600 mm or 200-500 mm lens is ideal. A teleconverter helps with small birds. 
- Use a high shutter speed (1/3200 s or more) for birds in flight. 
- Bean bags are provided in the vehicle. A small monopod is useful on the boats. 
Prefer a classic birding trip without the photography focus? See our 5 day Kenya birding safari or all birding safaris . For background reading, the Nature Kenya website has helpful notes on Kenya’s birds.

Day 1 : 
Nairobi to Lake Naivasha 

Drive from Nairobi into the Rift Valley, stopping at the viewpoint on the escarpment. After lunch at your lodge, take a boat ride on Lake Naivasha to photograph African fish eagles diving for fish, plus hippos, kingfishers and herons along the papyrus. 

Day 2 : 
Lake Naivasha to Lake Nakuru 

A short morning drive to Lake Nakuru National Park. Spend the day on the shoreline and in the acacia woodland, photographing flamingos, pelicans and storks, as well as rhinos, buffaloes and Rothschild's giraffes. 

Day 3 : 
Lake Nakuru to Lake Bogoria and Lake Baringo 

Drive north to Lake Bogoria, where steam rises from hot springs and flamingos feed on the soda water. Continue to Lake Baringo in the afternoon for your first birding walk around the lodge grounds. 

Day 4 : 
Lake Baringo boat ride and cliff walk 

Start with an early boat ride for herons, kingfishers, jacanas and fish eagles. Later, walk with a local bird guide to the cliffs and acacia scrub to look for owls, hornbills and colourful bush birds. Evening in the hide or around the lodge. 

Day 5 : 
Lake Baringo to Nairobi 

One more early morning of birding by the lake, then breakfast and the drive back to Nairobi, arriving in the late afternoon.

How many bird species can I see on this safari? 

Most guests see between 200 and 300 species in five days, depending on the season. Your guide keeps a list for you. 

Will I see flamingos? 

Very likely, but numbers change between lakes with the water levels. Your guide checks the latest reports and plans the route to give you the best chance. 

What lens do I need for the Kenya bird photography safari? 

A 150-600 mm or 200-500 mm zoom works best. Bring a teleconverter for small birds and a shorter zoom for flocks and landscapes. 

Are the boat rides included? 

Yes. The boat rides on Lake Naivasha and Lake Baringo are included, along with the park fees for Lake Nakuru and Lake Bogoria. 

When is the best time for bird photography in Kenya? 

October to April brings migrant birds and breeding colours. June to September is drier, with easier roads. Birding is rewarding all year. 

Can non-photographers join this trip? 

Yes. Partners and friends who enjoy nature will love it, and the pace includes rest time in the middle of the day. 

Can I extend the trip to the Masai Mara? 

Yes. The Mara is a short drive from Lake Naivasha. We can add two or three nights for big cats and more birds.
