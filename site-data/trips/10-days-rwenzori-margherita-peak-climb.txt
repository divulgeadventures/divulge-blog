## 
This Rwenzori Margherita Peak climb is a 10-day expedition in Uganda’s Rwenzori Mountains National Park. From Entebbe you drive to the mountains, trek the Central Circuit through Nyabitaba, John Matte, Bujuku and Elena, then climb glacier and rock to Margherita Peak (5,109 m), the third-highest summit in Africa. You return via Kitandara and Guy Yeoman camps. 
It suits adventurous trekkers with some experience using crampons, or who are happy to learn on the mountain with a guide. 

## Why choose the Rwenzori Margherita Peak climb? 
The Rwenzori, the legendary Mountains of the Moon, are unlike any other East African mountain: moss-draped forest, bogs, giant groundsels and lobelias, glacial lakes and permanent snow. The range is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and sees far fewer climbers than Kilimanjaro. 

## What to expect on the Rwenzori Margherita Peak climb 
Days 1 and 2 take you from Entebbe to the mountain base near Kasese. Days 3 to 6 climb through forest and bog to Elena Hut (about 4,540 m). Day 7 is summit day: glacier travel with crampons and a final rock section to Margherita Peak, then a descent to Kitandara. Days 8 and 9 return through Guy Yeoman to the base, and day 10 drives back to Entebbe. 

### When is the best time to climb? 
The driest months are December to February and June to August. Even then, expect mud and rain. Read about Uganda’s national parks and Kenya vs Uganda to plan a safari extension.

Day 1 : 
Arrive in Entebbe 

Arrive at Entebbe International Airport and transfer to your guesthouse in Entebbe. 

Day 2 : 
Entebbe to the Rwenzori 

Drive west, with lunch in Fort Portal, to a lodge at the base of the Rwenzori near Kasese. 

Day 3 : 
Trailhead to Nyabitaba Camp 

After park formalities, walk about 4 hours through forest to Nyabitaba Camp at about 2,650 m. 

Day 4 : 
Nyabitaba to John Matte Camp 

Cross the Bujuku River and climb through bamboo and heather, about 7 hours, to John Matte Camp at about 3,380 m. 

Day 5 : 
John Matte to Bujuku Camp 

Cross the Bigo bogs on boardwalks and muddy paths, 5 to 6 hours, to Bujuku Camp beside Lake Bujuku. A cold night. 

Day 6 : 
Bujuku to Elena Hut 

A shorter but steep day of about 5 hours to Elena Hut at about 4,540 m, below the glaciers. 

Day 7 : 
Margherita Peak summit, then Kitandara Camp 

Start before dawn, rope up on the Stanley Plateau glacier and climb to Margherita Peak (5,109 m). Descend to Kitandara Camp by its twin lakes, 8 to 10 hours or more. 

Day 8 : 
Kitandara to Guy Yeoman Camp 

Climb over Freshfield Pass and descend through bogs, 6 to 7 hours, to Guy Yeoman Camp. 

Day 9 : 
Guy Yeoman to the base 

Descend via Nyabitaba to the trailhead and transfer to your lodge or a hotel in Fort Portal. 

Day 10 : 
Drive to Entebbe 

Drive back to Entebbe for your flight home or an extra night.

How hard is the Rwenzori Margherita Peak climb? 

The Rwenzori Margherita Peak climb is one of East Africa’s toughest treks. Expect long days in mud and bogs, cold wet weather and a summit day with glacier travel, crampons and some rock scrambling. Good fitness and determination are essential. 

Do I need climbing experience? 

Basic experience with crampons and a harness is helpful, but your guide teaches the essentials. The final summit section involves roped glacier travel and short rock steps. 

When is the best time to climb the Rwenzori? 

The best months are December to February and June to August, when it is drier. The Rwenzori are wet all year, so good rain gear and gaiters are essential. 

What gear do I need? 

Bring waterproof clothing, gaiters or rubber boots for the bogs, warm layers and a sleeping bag. Crampons, a harness, a helmet and an ice axe are needed for the summit and can be rented locally. 

Can a group climb the Rwenzori together? 

Yes. Clubs and groups of friends often climb together. We plan enough guides for safe rope teams on the glacier. Contact us with your numbers and dates.
