## 
This gorilla photography safari is a short trip with a big reward. You travel from Kigali to Volcanoes National Park, trek to a habituated family of mountain gorillas and spend one hour photographing them in their forest home. 
A second trekking day with golden monkeys gives you another primate subject in the bamboo. It also gives you practice with the dim, green light of the forest. 

## Why choose this gorilla photography safari? 
Gorilla photography is very different from savannah photography. The light is low, the vegetation is thick and you are close to the animals. A little planning makes a big difference to your results. 
- One hour with the gorillas: silverbacks, mothers and playful youngsters. 
- Golden monkeys: fast, colourful and great practice for forest light. 
- Volcano views: the Virunga peaks above the farmland at sunrise. 
- Permits arranged: we secure your gorilla and golden monkey permits in advance. 

## Camera tips for the gorilla photography safari 
Flash is not allowed, so you will work in natural light. A fast lens or a camera that handles high ISO well is a great help. Many photographers use a 24-105 mm or 70-200 mm zoom, because the gorillas can be closer than you expect. 
- Set a higher ISO (1600 to 6400) and a shutter speed of 1/250 s or more. 
- Focus on the eyes, and use spot or zone focus to cut through the leaves. 
- Bring a rain cover and leave large bags with the porters. 

### Gorilla trekking rules 
Keep a distance of at least 7 metres, stay quiet and follow your ranger. Visits are limited to one hour to protect the gorillas. You must be at least 15 years old to trek. 

## Best time for a gorilla photography safari 
You can trek all year. June to September and December to February are drier, with easier trails. The wetter months bring lush green forest and soft light, and Rwanda often offers permit discounts in the low season. 
Read our guide to gorilla trekking in Rwanda and golden monkey trekking guide before you go. Official park details are on the Visit Rwanda Volcanoes National Park page .

Day 1 : 
Kigali to Volcanoes National Park 

Pick-up in Kigali and a scenic drive of about 2.5 hours to Musanze, with views of terraced hills and the Virunga volcanoes. Afternoon briefing on gorilla photography and time to prepare your gear. 

Day 2 : 
Gorilla trekking and photography 

Early transfer to the park headquarters at Kinigi for the briefing and group allocation. Trek through farmland and forest with your rangers to find your gorilla family, then spend one unforgettable hour photographing them. Return to the lodge to rest and review your images. 

Day 3 : 
Golden monkey trekking 

Trek into the bamboo forest to photograph golden monkeys as they feed and jump between stems. The afternoon is free for a community walk, a visit to the Twin Lakes or a second gorilla trek if you add one. 

Day 4 : 
Return to Kigali 

A relaxed breakfast with views of the volcanoes, then the drive back to Kigali for your onward flight or a city tour.

Can I use flash when photographing gorillas? 

No. Flash photography is not allowed with the gorillas. Use a high ISO and a fast lens to work with the natural forest light. 

What lens is best for a gorilla photography safari? 

A 24-105 mm or 70-200 mm zoom works well, because the gorillas are often close. A fast prime lens helps in dark forest. 

How long can I stay with the gorillas? 

Visits are limited to one hour once you reach the gorilla family. The trek to find them can take from 30 minutes to several hours. 

How fit do I need to be? 

You need moderate fitness for steep, muddy trails at altitude. Porters can carry your camera bag, and walking sticks are provided. 

Is there an age limit for gorilla trekking? 

Yes. You must be at least 15 years old to trek with the gorillas in Rwanda. 

When is the best time for gorilla photography? 

Trekking runs all year. The drier months make trails easier, while the green season gives lush scenery and fewer visitors. 

Can I trek the gorillas twice? 

Yes. A second gorilla trek can replace the golden monkey day if permits are available. It is a great way to get more images.
