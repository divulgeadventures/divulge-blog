## 
This Great Migration photography safari runs from July to October, when huge herds of wildebeest and zebra reach the Masai Mara. The highlight is the river crossing: thousands of animals plunging into the Mara River while crocodiles wait below. 
Crossings are unpredictable. A herd can gather on the bank for hours and then turn away. Four nights give you several full days at the river, which is the best way to come home with the shot. 

## Why choose this Great Migration photography safari? 
On a crossing day, patience wins. With a private vehicle you can stay at the river as long as you like, with a packed lunch and no one asking to leave. Your guide reads the herds and moves you to the crossing point with the best angle. 
- River crossings: leaping wildebeest, splashing water and crocodile strikes. 
- Big cats: lions and cheetahs follow the herds, so hunts are common. 
- Endless herds: lines of animals across the plains for wide-angle images. 
- Packed lunches: full days in the field without returning to camp. 

## How to photograph a river crossing 
Use a fast shutter speed, at least 1/2000 s, to freeze animals in mid-air. Shoot in bursts and keep both eyes open for the next jump. A zoom lens lets you switch between tight action and the full scene. 
Respect the rules at crossing points. Vehicles must keep back so the herds can cross, and a good guide never blocks their path. 

### Where you stay 
We choose camps near the Mara River so you spend less time driving to the crossing points. Ask us about tented camps or lodges to suit your budget. 

## Best time for a Great Migration photography safari 
The herds usually arrive in the Mara in July and stay until October. August and September are often the busiest months for crossings. Our Great Migration month by month guide and wildebeest migration guide explain the timing in detail.

When does the Great Migration photography safari run? 

It runs from July to October, when the herds are in the Masai Mara. August and September are usually the best months for river crossings. 

Is a river crossing guaranteed? 

No. Crossings depend on the herds and the weather. Four nights give you several days at the river, which greatly improves your chances. 

What shutter speed should I use for river crossings? 

Use at least 1/2000 s to freeze the action, shoot in bursts, and keep a zoom lens on your camera to switch between close-ups and wide scenes. 

Can I stay at the river all day? 

Yes. The vehicle is private, and we pack breakfast and lunch so you can stay as long as the herds are active. 

Are there many vehicles at the crossings? 

Crossing points can get busy in peak season. Your guide will look for quieter spots and the best angle, and always follows the reserve’s rules. 

What else can I photograph besides the migration? 

Lions, cheetahs, leopards, elephants, giraffes, hyenas and many birds. Predators are very active when the herds are around. 

Can I fly to the Masai Mara? 

Yes. Flights from Nairobi take about 45 minutes and save a long drive. We can arrange flights instead of road transfers.
