## 
This Kenya wildlife photography safari is for photographers who want variety. In seven days you move from the dry north to the green highlands, the Rift Valley and finally the open plains of the Masai Mara. 
Each park adds subjects you will not find in the others. That means a portfolio with real range, not a hundred pictures of the same scene. 

## Why choose this Kenya wildlife photography safari? 
Many safaris focus on one park. This one is planned like a photo assignment, with the timing of each drive matched to the light and to where the animals are most active. 
- Samburu: reticulated giraffes, Grevy’s zebras, gerenuks and Beisa oryx, the “Samburu Special Five”. 
- Ol Pejeta: black and white rhinos, with Mount Kenya behind them on clear mornings. 
- Lake Nakuru: flamingos, pelicans and rhinos along the shore. 
- Masai Mara: lions, cheetahs, leopards and endless open grassland. 

## Photography on the road 
Your private pop-up roof 4×4 has bean bags and charging points. Drives start early and end late, and the middle of the day is free for rest and editing. Your guide plans the route so you are rarely shooting into the sun. 

### Samburu and Ol Pejeta 
Samburu’s red soil, doum palms and the Ewaso Ng’iro River make warm, colourful backgrounds. At Ol Pejeta you can photograph rhinos at close range, and the conservancy is also home to chimpanzees at the Sweetwaters sanctuary. 

### Lake Nakuru and the Masai Mara 
Nakuru gives you water birds and rhinos in one frame. The last three days in the Mara are all about the cats, with time to wait for action and the chance of river crossings from July to October. 

## Best time for a Kenya wildlife photography safari 
June to October and January to February are the driest months, with easy roads and animals gathered near water. The green season brings fewer vehicles and dramatic skies, and Samburu stays dry for most of the year.

Day 1 : 
Nairobi to Samburu National Reserve 

Early pick-up in Nairobi and drive north past Mount Kenya to Samburu, arriving for lunch. Afternoon game drive along the Ewaso Ng'iro River, where elephants come to drink in the late light. 

Day 2 : 
Full day in Samburu 

Morning and afternoon drives in search of the Samburu Special Five, plus lions, leopards and elephants. Midday rest at the lodge with time to edit and back up your images. 

Day 3 : 
Samburu to Ol Pejeta Conservancy 

A sunrise drive in Samburu, then head south to Ol Pejeta. In the afternoon, photograph rhinos on the open plains, with Mount Kenya behind them if the sky is clear. 

Day 4 : 
Ol Pejeta to Lake Nakuru 

Morning drive in Ol Pejeta, then continue to Lake Nakuru National Park. Spend the afternoon on the shoreline photographing flamingos, pelicans and the park's rhinos. 

Day 5 : 
Lake Nakuru to the Masai Mara 

Early drive to the Masai Mara, arriving for lunch. Afternoon game drive on the plains, looking for lions and cheetahs in the golden light. 

Day 6 : 
Full day of big cat photography in the Masai Mara 

A full day in the reserve with a packed breakfast and lunch, so you can stay with the animals when the action starts. An optional sunrise balloon flight can be added. 

Day 7 : 
Sunrise drive and return to Nairobi 

One last sunrise game drive, then breakfast and the drive back to Nairobi, arriving in the late afternoon. A short flight back is also possible.

Which parks does the Kenya wildlife photography safari visit? 

It visits Samburu National Reserve, Ol Pejeta Conservancy, Lake Nakuru National Park and the Masai Mara National Reserve. 

What are the Samburu Special Five? 

They are the reticulated giraffe, Grevy’s zebra, Beisa oryx, gerenuk and Somali ostrich. All five are adapted to the dry north and are rare in southern Kenya. 

How much driving is there? 

The longest drive is from Lake Nakuru to the Masai Mara, about 5 to 6 hours with stops. We can replace some drives with short flights if you prefer. 

What camera gear should I bring? 

Bring a long zoom such as 150-600 mm, a mid-range zoom for landscapes and herds, two bodies if you have them, plenty of batteries and a dust blower. 

Is this safari suitable for beginners? 

Yes. Your guide helps with positioning and timing, and the pace leaves time to learn. Experienced photographers enjoy the flexibility too. 

When is the best time to go? 

June to October and January to February are the driest months. The green season from November to May has fewer vehicles and beautiful skies. 

Can I add a balloon flight in the Masai Mara? 

Yes. A sunrise balloon flight can be added on day 6 at an extra cost.
