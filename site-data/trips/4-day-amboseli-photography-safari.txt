## 
This Amboseli photography safari gives you three nights in one of Kenya’s most photogenic parks. Amboseli is famous for large elephant families, many with long tusks, set against Mount Kilimanjaro across the border in Tanzania. 
Three nights matter here. The mountain is often hidden by cloud, and the clearest views usually come at dawn. More mornings in the park mean more chances of the shot you came for. 

## Why choose this Amboseli photography safari? 
Amboseli is flat and open, which makes it easy to find animals and to frame them cleanly. The swamps fed by Kilimanjaro’s underground water keep elephants, buffaloes and hippos close to the roads all year. 
- Elephants below Kilimanjaro: the image most photographers come for. 
- Swamp scenes: elephants half-submerged in green water and egrets at their feet. 
- Dust and drama: herds walking through dust in low, golden light. 
- Birds: pelicans, crowned cranes and flamingos on the seasonal lake. 

## What a day looks like 
Mornings start early to catch the mountain before the clouds build. You return to the lodge for brunch and a rest, then head out again in the afternoon. Clouds often lift late in the day, and Kilimanjaro can appear again just before sunset. 

### Shooting tips for Amboseli 
- Use a mid-range zoom (70-200 mm) to fit elephants and the mountain in one frame. 
- Try a low angle from the open window for elephants towering over the horizon. 
- Bring a cloth to cover your camera, as Amboseli is very dusty in the dry season. 

## Best time for an Amboseli photography safari 
June to October and January to February are the driest months, with the best chance of clear mountain views. The short rains in November and the long rains from March to May bring green grass and dramatic skies, with fewer vehicles.

Day 1 : 
Nairobi to Amboseli National Park 

Pick-up in Nairobi and drive south to Amboseli, arriving for lunch at your lodge. In the afternoon, set out on your first game drive towards the swamps, where elephant families gather as the light turns golden. 

Day 2 : 
Sunrise and sunset photography in Amboseli 

Leave before dawn for the best chance of a clear Kilimanjaro behind the elephants. Return for brunch and a rest during the middle of the day. In the afternoon, explore the dusty plains and the edge of the seasonal lake for pelicans and flamingos. 

Day 3 : 
A full day following the elephant herds 

Another early start, this time following herds as they walk from the woodland to the swamps. Your guide positions the vehicle for low angles and clean backgrounds. Stay out until the park closes for sunset silhouettes. 

Day 4 : 
Final sunrise drive and return to Nairobi 

One last sunrise drive for the mountain, then breakfast and the drive back to Nairobi, arriving in the afternoon.

How long is the drive from Nairobi to Amboseli? 

The drive takes about 4 to 5 hours. You can also fly in about 45 minutes, which gives you an extra afternoon game drive. 

Will I see Kilimanjaro on the Amboseli photography safari? 

It is very likely but never guaranteed. The mountain is clearest at sunrise and sometimes again just before sunset. That is why this trip has three mornings in the park. 

What lens is best for Amboseli? 

A 70-200 mm lens is great for elephants with the mountain behind them. Add a 100-400 mm for close portraits and birds, and a wide lens for landscapes. 

Is Amboseli good for photography all year? 

Yes. The dry months give the clearest mountain views and dusty, golden scenes. The rainy months bring green grass and fewer vehicles. 

Are there big cats in Amboseli? 

Lions and cheetahs live in Amboseli, but sightings are less frequent than in the Masai Mara. Elephants are the main subject here. 

Can I combine Amboseli with the Masai Mara? 

Yes. Many photographers spend three nights in Amboseli and then fly or drive to the Masai Mara for big cats. We can plan the full route for you. 

Is the safari vehicle shared? 

No. Your pop-up roof 4×4 is private, so you set the pace at each sighting.
